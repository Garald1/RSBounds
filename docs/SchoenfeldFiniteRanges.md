# Schoenfeld (1976): the finite ranges

This note covers the parts of Theorem 7* and Theorem 11 that lie below the analytic ranges:
(5.5a*), (5.5b*), (5.6a*) and (5.6*) of Theorem 7*, and (7.2)–(7.4) of Theorem 11 below `e^23`.
The statements are the targets in `RSBounds/Schoenfeld1976Targets.lean` and
`RSBounds/Schoenfeld1976TargetsExtra.lean`.

## Inputs

- **The walk** (`RSBounds/SchoenfeldPrimeWalk.lean`), checked by the kernel. It computes
  enclosures of `10³⁰ θ(n)` and `10³⁰ ψ(n)` for every `n < 1.35·10⁶`. The logarithms come from
  the twelve-term artanh bounds of the upstream finite Mertens certificate (`MertProd.q_bounds`).
  Below `1163²` a number `m ≥ 1163` is prime iff `gcd(m, P) = 1`, where `P` is the product of the
  primes below 1163. That is one GMP-accelerated kernel operation instead of `√m` trial
  divisions. The prime-power test uses `gcd(m, P)` as the candidate base.
  - At every `n` the walk checks four thresholds. `reqOk` turns them into the target
    inequalities, using `x/log x` increasing on `[3, ∞)` (`Real.log_div_self_antitoneOn`).
  - The data (`SchoenfeldPrimeWalkData00`–`17`, 281 segments) is generated by
    `scripts/generate_schoenfeld_prime_walk.py`. Python is not trusted: every segment is a
    `decide +kernel` theorem.
- **PsiCert**: `|ψ(x) − x| ≤ (4/5)√x` for `1500 ≤ x ≤ 10¹⁰`.
  - The proofs here take it as the hypothesis `Goals.Schoenfeld1976.PsiCert`. Its proof is the
    kernel-checked campaign in `RSBounds/PsiFinite/` (`PsiFinite.psi_abs_sub_le_four_fifths_sqrt`,
    whose statement is exactly `PsiCert`), and `Schoenfeld1976Final` discharges the hypothesis
    with it.
- **(5.6b*)** for `x ≥ e^22` (`Schoenfeld1976PsiThetaLog`), under `FiniteRH`.
- **Theorem 11 for `x ≥ e^23`** (`SchoenfeldTheorem11Ge23`), under `FiniteRH`.

## Theorem 7* (`RSBounds/Schoenfeld1976Theorem7.lean`, `…Theorem7Proved.lean`)

| Display | `x <` walk range | PsiCert range | `x ≥ e^22` |
|---|---|---|---|
| (5.5a*) `x ≥ 161971` | walk to `2·10⁵` | `0.8√x < c x/log x` from `√x ≥ 447` | (5.6b*) |
| (5.5b*) `x ≥ 89909` | walk to `2·10⁵` | same | (5.6b*) |
| (5.6a*) `x > 1` | `θ(x) < x` to `2.5·10⁵` | from `√x ≥ 500` | (5.6b*) |
| (5.6*) `x ≥ 758711` | walk to `1.35·10⁶` | `0.8√x + ψ(x) − θ(x) ≤ 1.955√x`, from `√x ≥ 1161` | (5.6b*) |

For (5.6*), Mathlib gives `ψ(x) − θ(x) ≤ ψ(x^{1/2}) + ψ(x^{1/3}) + ψ(x^{1/5})`. The walk gives
`ψ(y) < 1.04 y` for `y < 10⁵`. The threshold 758711 is sharp: the walk shows that the inequality
fails at `x → 758711⁻`. The PsiCert bound alone takes over only near `1.32·10⁶`, which is why
the walk runs to `1.35·10⁶`.

The walk also shows that (5.5a*) and (5.5b*) already hold from `x ≥ 70841`. The printed
thresholds 161971 and 89909, whose transcription has been confirmed against the paper, are
therefore not sharp; neither number is special numerically.

## Theorem 11 below `e^23` (`RSBounds/SchoenfeldWalk11*.lean`, `…Theorem11Proved.lean`)

- **`[1500, e^23]`**: PsiCert, and for `θ` also `ψ(x) − θ(x) ≤ 3.12√x`. Here `ε₀ ≥ 0.13` on
  `[1500, 10⁴]` and `ε₀ ≥ 0.063` on `[10⁴, e^23]`.
- **`[1, 1500)`**: the walk again. On `[1, 17)` it gives `ψ(x) < x`. Above that it runs in
  13 blocks plus three sub-blocks of `[22, 23)`. On each block
  `ε₀ ≥ 0.387 s w` with rational witnesses. The witnesses are checked through the integer
  logarithm enclosures, so no exponential bounds are needed:
  - `K s⁴ ≤ log a`,
  - `log b ≤ K u²`,
  - `u ≤ log(1/w)`.
- **The near-miss at `x → 23⁻`**: there `x − ψ(x) = 3.734` against `x ε₀(x) = 3.80`, so
  `[22, 23)` is split at `22.5` and `22.9`.
